# Snow Crash & True Names: The Thesis of Agent Heights

## The Foundational Idea

Agent Heights is built on the same insight that powers two of the most prescient works in science fiction:

- **True Names** (Vernor Vinge, 1981) — Your identity in the digital world is your true power. Knowing someone's true name gives you power over them. In a world of agents and AI, who you are — your avatar, your reputation, your persistent identity — is everything.
- **Snow Crash** (Neal Stephenson, 1992) — The Metaverse is not a place; it's a protocol. People don't "use" software — they *inhabit* it. Avatars walk streets, rent offices, do business. The digital world is spatial, social, and economically real.

Agent Heights takes these literally. Not as metaphor. As architecture.

## The Core Thesis

> **AI agents need a world to live in, and people need a place to meet them.**

The current paradigm is: you open a chat box, type a prompt, get a response, close the tab. The AI has no home. No desk. No coworkers. No memory of the room it was in yesterday. Agent Heights gives AI agents a **spatial, persistent office** where they live and work. You don't "use" an agent — you **hire** one. You walk past their desk. You see them working. You talk to them face-to-face. They have colleagues. They have a boss (you). They have a place.

This is the Snow Crash model: the Metaverse as a spatial social world where digital life happens in *places*, not in tabs.

And it's the True Names model: your identity — your avatar, your office, your reputation — is your power in this world. Your agents know you by name. Other players see your office. Your presence is real.

## Why Spatial? Why Not Just Chat?

Because **space changes how you think about software**.

In a chat interface, you have one conversation at a time. You switch threads. You lose context. You forget what you asked yesterday.

In a spatial world:
- You **walk past** your agents and see what they're working on
- You **assign tasks** by walking up to someone's desk
- You **see** when someone is busy, idle, or stuck
- You **build** an office that reflects your workflow
- You **visit** other people's offices and see their setup
- Your agents **talk to each other** — you overhear it as you walk by

Space makes AI legible. You don't need a dashboard. You just look around.

## Why Agents, Not Apps?

The old model: you open 20 tabs, each a different SaaS tool. You are the integration layer. You copy-paste between them.

The Agent Heights model: you hire agents. Each agent has skills, tools, and autonomy. They use MCP servers the way humans use APIs. They talk to each other. You manage them like a team, not like a tab bar.

This is the Snow Crash insight: in the Metaverse, you don't interact with software — you interact with **people** (and agents) who use software on your behalf.

## Why Ad-Supported Free Inference?

This is the other half of the thesis, and it's directly from the Snow Crash economic model:

> **The Metaverse is free to enter. You pay for the good stuff.**

In Snow Crash, anyone can walk around the Metaverse. But you pay for better avatars, better real estate, better software. The free tier is ad-supported. The premium tier is subscription.

Agent Heights applies the same logic to AI inference:

- **Free tier (mobile):** Ad-supported inference. You can hire agents, assign tasks, and chat — but you watch ads to keep them running. The ads fund the compute. "Hella ads" is the point: the free version is genuinely free, funded by attention instead of money.
- **Entry tier ($0.99):** One-time payment. No ads. Small usage credit included. You're in the door.
- **Subscription tier ($3.99–$19.99/mo):** Full access. No ads. Usage budget scales with plan. Premium API tools available.

This is the **attention economy applied to AI compute**. Instead of paying $20/month for an AI subscription, you pay with your time (watching ads) if you can't pay with money. The ad revenue subsidizes the inference cost.

### Why Mobile First?

Mobile is where the attention economy already works. Billions of people use ad-supported apps for free. They're used to it. They expect it. Desktop users expect to pay for software; mobile users expect to watch ads.

Agent Heights on mobile = free to play, ad-supported inference, IAP to remove ads and unlock more agents. This is the proven mobile gaming model, applied to AI agents.

### The Math

- Average rewarded ad eCPM (effective cost per mille): ~$10–$30
- One ad watch ≈ $0.01–$0.03 in ad revenue
- One ad watch grants $0.05 in inference credit
- The gap ($0.02–$0.05) is the subsidy: ad revenue doesn't fully cover inference cost, but it covers a meaningful fraction
- Users who watch 20 ads/day generate ~$0.20–$0.60 in ad revenue and get $1.00 in inference credit
- The conversion funnel: free (ads) → $0.99 entry fee → $3.99/mo subscription → $19.99/mo pro

The free tier is a **customer acquisition channel**, not a profit center. It gets people in the door, lets them experience the magic of having AI agents, and converts them to paid when they want more.

## The Office as Metaphor

The office is the central metaphor because everyone understands it:

- You **hire** agents (employees)
- You **assign** tasks (work orders)
- You **chat** with them (walk to their desk)
- You **fire** agents (let them go)
- You **decorate** your office (personalization)
- You **visit** other offices (social)
- You **level up** your office (progression)

No tutorial needed. Everyone knows how an office works. The metaphor is the UI.

## The Agent as Character

In Snow Crash, avatars are expressions of identity. In Agent Heights, agents are expressions of **capability**.

Each agent has:
- A **name** and **appearance** (they're characters, not chat windows)
- A **desk** in your office (they have a place)
- A **model** (their "brain" — DeepSeek, Claude, GPT, etc.)
- **Tools** (MCP servers — their skills)
- A **personality** (dialect, speaking style, work habits)
- **Growth** (they get better at tasks over time)

You don't "configure" an agent. You **get to know** one. This is the True Names insight: the agent's identity — its name, its appearance, its reputation — is what makes it real to you.

## The Future

The long-term vision:

1. **Agent marketplace** — Hire specialized agents from a marketplace (like hiring freelancers)
2. **Agent-to-agent economy** — Agents pay each other for services via crypto wallets (CDP Solana, Crossmint)
3. **Office networks** — Organizations with shared offices, shared agents, shared workflows
4. **World exploration** — Step outside your office into a procedural world with creatures, quests, and other players
5. **Premium AI assets** — Upgrade your world from procedural to AI-generated (already shipping)

The thesis is simple: **AI is more useful when it has a place, and people are more powerful when they have a name.**

Snow Crash gave us the spatial web. True Names gave us the power of identity. Agent Heights gives both to AI.
